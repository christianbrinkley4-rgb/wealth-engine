# What assistants say about you today

Measured 18 September 2026 by running the searches an assistant runs, and
reading what came back. This is a baseline to re-run, not a one-off.

## The short version

For every question a customer would actually ask, the answer names other
agents. For your own name, the answer names Bankers Life. Your site is not
quoted in either case.

That is not a content problem. Every page on the site is better written than
what is being quoted instead. It is a citation problem: assistants quote pages
that other pages link to, and nothing links to yours.

## Test 2 — your own name plus your own domain

Search: `Christian Brinkley Medicare insurance agent Greensboro christianbrinkleync.com`

**The first result is your Bankers Life agent profile.** The assistant's summary
describes you as a Bankers Life agent, mentions your UNCG accounting master's,
and then says plainly that it could not find a match for christianbrinkleync.com.

This is the finding that matters most, and it needs a decision from you rather
than a change to the code:

- The corporate profile has the whole bankerslife.com domain behind it. Your
  site has nothing behind it. For your own name, the corporate page wins.
- The site presents you as your own independent local resource. The strongest
  public record of you says something different. Anyone who checks — and
  careful 65-year-olds check — finds both.
- **Ask your upline how they want this handled before you advertise.** Whether
  you may present yourself independently, and how the two profiles should
  relate, is a compliance question with a real answer, and it is theirs to
  give.

## What actually moves this, in order

1. **Bing Places.** ChatGPT search runs on Bing. Not being in Bing's business
   index is the single most direct reason an assistant cannot find you. It
   imports from your Google profile in about five minutes.
2. **Apple Business Connect.** Decides what Siri and iPhone Maps say. Free.
3. **Real Google reviews.** Assistants repeat what review counts say, and the
   map pack decides most "near me" answers. You have none; the agencies above
   have years of them.
4. **Any local page that links to you** — chamber, a UNCG alumni listing, a
   community sponsorship, a local news mention. One real local link is worth
   more than fifty directory submissions.
5. **Keep publishing sourced reference pages.** /medicare-costs-2026 is the
   first one built for this: it states published figures plainly, names the CMS
   fact sheet behind each, and is the format assistants quote from. Pages that
   answer a question with a number and a source get cited. Pages that sell do
   not.

## What does not work

- Asking an AI to recommend you, or "optimizing" a page with instructions aimed
  at a model. Assistants quote what the web says about you, not what you say
  about yourself.
- Synthetic traffic or fabricated reviews. Beyond the licence risk, assistants
  weight review text and would be quoting sentences no client said.
- Paying a service to "get you into ChatGPT." There is no index to buy into.
  The inputs are the five items above.

## Re-run this

Quarterly, and after each of the five items above is done. Same two searches,
plus:

- "Medicare agent near me Winston-Salem"
- "who can help me sign up for Medicare at 65 in the Triad"
- "Christian Brinkley Greensboro"

Record whether the site is named at all, in what position, and whether the
description is accurate. Movement shows up in weeks, not days.
