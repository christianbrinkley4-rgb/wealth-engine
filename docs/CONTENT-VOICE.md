# Writing for Christian’s visitors

The audience is people approaching retirement, people who are already retired, and family members helping them. Write as Christian speaking with one person who has a question. The tone should be warm, respectful, calm, and easy to understand.

## What to make clear

- Christian is a local person visitors can get to know and return to throughout retirement.
- Consultations are no cost, with no obligation to enroll or buy. Visitors can take time to decide.
- Meetings can be at home, at a convenient public location, or by phone. A spouse or family member is welcome.
- Questions and requests go directly to Christian and are not sold to other agents.
- Christian is currently a licensed insurance agent and an accounting master’s student at UNCG. He works with an advisor for financial planning. Future CPA, CFP, tax, and investment credentials must not be presented as current qualifications.

## How to write

Use familiar words: “doctors,” “monthly costs,” “when to enroll,” and “questions you’d like to discuss.” Explain unfamiliar insurance terms when they first appear. Use complete, conversational questions in FAQs. Keep sentences and paragraphs manageable without talking down to readers.

Describe what visitors can do, what they can learn, and what happens next. Use local details when they help someone make a decision, such as checking a doctor or confirming Medicare Advantage availability for their address. Service-area copy should simply name the communities and explain ways to meet.

Avoid website-building language about templates, SEO, landing pages, funnels, databases, lead quality, or automation. Those subjects belong in internal documentation. Do not describe communities as “first-class,” “inside the radius,” or “not the outer edge.”

Avoid forced kitchen-table metaphors, competitive attacks, pressure about age, and claims that “most people” should make a particular choice. A real home visit is useful to mention; it should not become a repeated slogan. Do not invent client stories, quotes from customers, statistics, service guarantees, or response times.

Keep factual explanations balanced. Do not promise that a plan covers a doctor, that a premium will fall, or that an application will be accepted before checking the relevant details. Financial calculators should state the rates used and describe results as estimates. For changing Medicare, Social Security, tax, or insurance rules, check an official primary source.

Only describe services that work today. A request form does not reserve an appointment. Saving dates to a personal calendar does not mean Christian sends reminder emails. Explain connected services accurately when they become available.

## Example

“Personal help close to home. I serve Greensboro, High Point, Winston-Salem, and nearby communities. We can meet at your home or talk by phone. Bring your questions, and we’ll take them one at a time.”

## Sounding like a real person, not AI (added September 21, 2026)

Christian’s standing rule for everything public — social posts, captions, ads, text, photos, tags: nothing may look AI-generated.

- **Text:** No em dashes (use hyphens or commas). Short, uneven sentences. Contractions. No marketing fluff (“once-a-year chance,” “that sort of thing,” “game-changer,” “in today’s world,” “delve”). Write like a 21-year-old local talking to a neighbor, cleaned up one notch. Keep his real lines (“No call centers, just me”).
- **Photos:** AI-generated is fine as long as it’s not AI slop. The bar: quality and professional for a T65 audience — clean, trustworthy, warm, readable. No distorted text, no uncanny faces, no meme energy, nothing with the generic “AI look.” His real headshot for anything personal or face-forward; branded navy/gold graphics for covers and post images. (Yelp cover reviewed Sep 21, 2026 — passes, keeping it.)
- **Tags:** No hashtag blocks. On Nextdoor and Facebook, skip hashtags entirely or use at most one plain one. Real neighbors don’t hashtag.
- **Substance:** Local specifics over generic claims — Triad, Greensboro, Creedmoor, real dates (Oct 15 – Dec 7). Never invent experience, clients, reviews, stats, or guarantees.
- **Every post and ad drives to the website first.** The site is what separates Christian from other agents — it’s the proof he’s local and knows his stuff. Phone number stays as backup (the 65+ crowd calls), but the site is the lead call to action. (Standing rule from Christian, Sep 21, 2026 — apply without being asked.)
