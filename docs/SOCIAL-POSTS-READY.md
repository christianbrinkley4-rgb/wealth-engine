## 1. Facebook — personal profile

The best-performing version of this post is not an advertisement. It is you
telling people you know what you have been doing. Your friends' parents are the
audience, not your friends.

> Something I've been working on for a while is finally up.
>
> I kept having the same conversation with people turning 65 — they'd get a
> stack of mail eight inches high, every envelope looked official, and not one
> of them explained what the deadlines actually were or what happens if you
> miss them.
>
> So I built the thing I wished I could hand them. Enrollment dates you can
> work out in about a minute, what the penalties actually cost, and every 2026
> Medicare figure in one place with the government source next to it. No
> account, no email required, nothing to sign up for.
>
> christianbrinkleync.com
>
> If you've got a parent in the Triad who's coming up on 65 and looks
> overwhelmed by it, send it to them. And if they'd rather just ask a person,
> that's what I'm here for — I'll sit down at the kitchen table and go through
> it with them.

**Why it reads like this:** no plan names, no benefit claims, no "call now."
It's a story about a problem you saw, which is what people share. The ask at
the end is to forward it to a parent — that reaches your actual buyer through
someone who already trusts you.

## 3. Nextdoor

**Read this first: don't post it to the neighborhood feed from your personal
account.** Nextdoor's guidelines send business self-promotion to a free
Business Page, and promotional posts in the main feed get flagged and removed
by neighbors. Claim the page first (free, at business.nextdoor.com) — that also
gives you the listing and the link, which is the part that helps you.

Then post this from the Business Page:

> Hi neighbors — I'm Christian, I'm a licensed insurance agent here in
> Greensboro, and I help people in the Triad figure out Medicare when they turn 65.
>
> I'm not a call center and I don't work off a lead list. I meet people at
> their kitchen table, go through their situation, and when something's outside
> what I handle, I tell them who can actually help.
>
> I've put the free tools on my site — enrollment dates, what the late
> penalties cost, and the 2026 Medicare figures with their government sources.
> No email required to use any of it: christianbrinkleync.com
>
> Happy to answer a question here for free, whether or not you ever call me.
>
> Not connected with or endorsed by the U.S. government or the federal Medicare
> program.

**The highest-value thing on Nextdoor is not this post.** It's answering the
Medicare questions neighbors already ask, helpfully, without pitching. Do that
four or five times and people start tagging you when someone else asks. That is
how a local agent wins on Nextdoor.

## Where these actually help, honestly

Facebook and Nextdoor links are `nofollow` — they pass no ranking signal to
Google, so these posts will not fix the "no referring pages" problem in Search
Console. What they do:

- Put the site in front of the people most likely to forward it to a parent.
- Give Bing and the assistants that read it another place your name, phone
  number and domain appear together, which is how an entity gets resolved.
- Give you a Nextdoor Business Page, which is a real local listing.

The links that move Google are still the ones in
[LOCAL-SEO-CITATIONS.md](LOCAL-SEO-CITATIONS.md) — chamber, community, local
press.

## The one gate before any of this goes out

You asked me this morning why your own name pulls up a Bankers Life profile
instead of your site. This is the same question, arriving in a form that is
public and permanent.

A Medicare post on social media from an appointed agent is marketing. Most
uplines require it to be reviewed before it's used, and the disclaimer
requirements are not optional. Posting independent-agent marketing publicly,
before you know where your upline stands on you presenting independently, is
exactly the exposure I flagged in [AI-VISIBILITY.md](AI-VISIBILITY.md).

One message to your upline covers all of it:

> I've built a personal website for my Medicare business —
> christianbrinkleync.com. I'd like to post about it on Facebook and Nextdoor.
> Two questions: does anything I publish need to go through review first, and
> is there anything about how I present myself on it that you need changed?

Their answer takes a day and settles all three posts above.
