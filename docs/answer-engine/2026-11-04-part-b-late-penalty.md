# The Medicare Part B late penalty: how it's calculated and how to avoid it

If you could have signed up for Medicare Part B and didn't, your monthly premium can go up by 10% for each full 12-month period you went without it. In most cases you keep paying that extra amount for as long as you have Part B. The penalty is figured on the standard premium, which is $202.90 a month in 2026. So a two-year gap adds $40.58 a month, every month.

## How the math works

Medicare looks at how long you could have had Part B but didn't have it. Then it does three things:

1. Counts the full 12-month periods in that gap. Partial years don't count.
2. Multiplies the number of full periods by 10%.
3. Applies that percentage to the standard Part B premium for the current year.

Here's the example Medicare.gov uses. Someone waits 24 months to sign up and doesn't qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. That's two full 12-month periods, so the penalty is 20%.

- 20% of $202.90 = $40.58 extra a month
- $202.90 + $40.58 = $243.48, which Medicare rounds to $243.50 a month

Now a longer gap. A CMS training example looks at someone who went 6 years and 4 months without Part B. That's six full periods. The extra four months are dropped. The penalty is 60%. At 2026 rates, 60% of $202.90 is $121.74 extra a month.

Notice what that means. Waiting 11 months longer than a full year doesn't add anything. Waiting one more month to finish a 12-month period does.

## The dollar amount moves every year

The penalty is a percentage, not a fixed dollar amount. It rides on top of whatever the standard premium is that year. When the premium goes up, your penalty in dollars goes up too, even though the percentage stays the same.

CMS usually announces next year's premium in the fall. When it changes, the percentages stay the same. Just swap in the new standard premium.

## It's based on the standard premium, not your income

Some people with higher incomes pay more for Part B. That's a separate charge, often called IRMAA. The late penalty is figured on the standard premium only.

## When you won't owe the penalty

You generally won't owe the penalty if one of these applies.

**You had job-based coverage from current work.** If you or your spouse are still working and you're covered by that employer's group health plan, you can usually delay Part B. When the job or the coverage ends, whichever comes first, you get an 8-month Special Enrollment Period to sign up without a penalty. More on that at [/special-enrollment](/special-enrollment).

**Careful: COBRA and retiree coverage don't count as current work.** This is the big trap. Medicare doesn't treat COBRA as group coverage based on current employment, and a retiree health plan isn't either. Going on COBRA also doesn't extend your 8-month window. People who retire, take COBRA, and wait can end up with a penalty they never saw coming.

**You missed your window for a reason Medicare recognizes.** Social Security lists exceptional situations that can qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, including a natural disaster or emergency, incarceration, an employer or health plan error, and losing Medicaid coverage.

**You qualify for a Medicare Savings Program.** These state-run programs help people with limited income pay Medicare costs. Social Security says the penalty doesn't continue if you're eligible for one.

**You got Medicare through disability before 65.** If you receive Social Security disability benefits, Social Security says the penalty stops once you reach 65.

## If you already missed your window

If your sign-up window has passed and none of the exceptions fit, there's still the General Enrollment Period. It runs January 1 to March 31 each year. Your Part B coverage starts the month after you sign up. A late penalty can still apply when you use it.

First, check whether a Special Enrollment Period fits. It can change your start date and whether you owe a penalty at all.

## How to avoid it in the first place

- **Know your first window.** Your Initial Enrollment Period is seven months around your 65th birthday. The full timeline is at [/turning-65](/turning-65).
- **If you're working past 65, confirm your coverage counts.** It needs to be a group health plan tied to current employment, yours or your spouse's. Ask your benefits office how your plan works with Medicare.
- **Keep proof.** When you sign up for Part B later through a Special Enrollment Period, Social Security asks for proof of your job-based coverage. Their [Part B only sign-up page](https://www.ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up/part-b-only) lists what they need.
- **Don't treat COBRA as a waiting room.** If your job ends, the 8-month clock starts then.
- **Remember Part D is separate.** Drug coverage has its own late penalty with its own rules. Avoiding one doesn't mean you've avoided the other.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the Medicare Part B late enrollment penalty?

It's 10% of the standard Part B premium for each full 12-month period you could have had Part B but didn't. In 2026 the standard premium is $202.90 a month, so each full year of delay adds $20.29 a month.

### Do I have to pay the Part B penalty for the rest of my life?

In most cases you pay it for as long as you have Part B. Social Security names two exceptions: if you're eligible for a Medicare Savings Program, or if you get Social Security disability benefits and you reach 65.

### Does COBRA count as coverage to avoid the Part B penalty?

No. Medicare doesn't treat COBRA as group health coverage based on current employment, and retiree coverage doesn't count either. Your 8-month Special Enrollment Period starts when the job or the job-based coverage ends, whichever comes first, and COBRA doesn't extend it.

### Is the late penalty higher if my income is higher?

No. The penalty is figured on the standard premium only. Higher-income people may pay an extra income-related amount for Part B, but that's a separate charge.

### I'm still working and have insurance through my job. Will I get a penalty if I wait?

Usually not, if your coverage is a group health plan based on current employment, yours or your spouse's. When that job or coverage ends, you have 8 months to sign up without a penalty.

### What if I already missed my enrollment window?

You can sign up during the General Enrollment Period, January 1 to March 31, and coverage starts the month after you sign up. A penalty may apply. First check whether you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, which can avoid the penalty.
