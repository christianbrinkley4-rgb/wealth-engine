## Tier 2: local links, which is what's actually missing

A handful of genuine local links beats a hundred directory submissions. Aim for ten over the next two months.

- **Greensboro Chamber of Commerce** ([greensboro.org/membership](https://greensboro.org/membership/)) — 1,300+ members, and membership includes a directory listing that links to your site. It costs money, so treat it as a business decision, not just an SEO one; the referrals and the local credibility are the real return.
- **Better Business Bureau of Central North Carolina** — an accredited listing is a strong local citation and the sort of thing this audience checks.
- **Alignable** — free, local-business focused, and gives a followed link. No Christian Brinkley Greensboro profile found (18 Sep 2026 hunt). Create only when ready; do not wire a URL until it exists and is not carrier-branded.
- **Your church, and any club, gym, or group you belong to** — member and sponsor pages are exactly the kind of local link Google trusts and competitors can't copy.
- **UNCG connections** — student organizations and alumni directories often list members' businesses. Use only what's genuinely yours, and never imply the university endorses your practice.
- **Public library and senior center event pages** — when you run a "Turning 65 in the Triad" talk, the venue lists the event, with a link. This is the best one on the list: it produces a link, an audience, and appointments at the same time.
- **Local news and community calendars** — the Rhino Times, Triad Business Journal community calendar, and neighborhood newsletters list free educational events.

## Tier 3: insurance directories, with a caveat

Sites like medicareagentshub.com and certifiedmedicareagents.com will list you, and they do pass a link. Read what they do with your information first: several of them exist to resell leads, which is the opposite of the promise your site makes. If a listing requires handing over prospects, skip it. A link is not worth contradicting the one thing that makes you different.

## Reviews: the highest-value thing you can do this week

Reviews decide the order of the map pack, and the map pack is what most "medicare agent near me" searches actually show. You have none. Competitors with forty will outrank a perfect website indefinitely.

**Public write-review link** (independent listing Place ID `ChIJCYLxNHVn4U0ReVLReD8wwqo`):

https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=ChIJCYLxNHVn4U0ReVLReD8wwqo

- Ask every client you've already helped. Start with the ones whose problem you actually solved.
- Ask in person or by phone, then send the link above. Owner “Ask for reviews” in Google Business Profile Manager also needs Christian’s Google login.
- Never offer anything in exchange — it violates Google's policies and CMS marketing rules, and it's the kind of thing that costs an insurance license.
- Specific beats glowing: "He found out my cardiologist wasn't in the plan I was about to pick" persuades a stranger. "Great service" doesn't.

What to say:

> I'm building up my Google reviews, and yours would carry real weight with other families around here. Would you mind writing a couple of sentences about what we worked on? Here's the link: https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=ChIJCYLxNHVn4U0ReVLReD8wwqo

## What not to do

- Don't buy links or pay for "500 directory submissions." Google discounts them at best.
- Don't create a second Google profile. Duplicates get suspended.
- Don't vary the phone number or business name between listings, even slightly.
- Don't write your own reviews or ask staff to. That's the one mistake that can end a listing permanently.
