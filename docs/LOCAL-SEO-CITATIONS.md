# Getting found: listings, links, and reviews

Google Search Console reports **"Referring page: None detected"** for christianbrinkleync.com. Nothing on the internet links to it. That, not the website itself, is what's holding back rankings — the pages are well built, they just have no votes behind them.

This is the work that fixes it. None of it needs a developer. Most of it is free.

## First, the details that must match everywhere

Every listing has to carry exactly the same three things, character for character. Inconsistency is what makes Google unsure two listings are the same business.

```
Christian Brinkley
Greensboro, NC (service area — no street address shown)
(336) 365-7422
https://christianbrinkleync.com
```

Category everywhere: **Insurance agent**. Never publish the old 919 number again.

Short description to paste when a site asks for one:

> Licensed insurance agent in Greensboro serving the Piedmont Triad. Medicare help for people approaching 65, plus life insurance, long-term care coverage, and retirement income questions. Meet at your home, nearby, or by phone. No cost, no obligation.

## Tier 1: the free profiles that matter most (about two hours total)

Do these in order. Each one is both a link and a place people search.

1. **Google Business Profile** — done and verified. Description, website, phone, category and service areas are set.
2. **Bing Places** — bingplaces.com. It can import directly from your Google profile, which takes about five minutes. Bing also feeds ChatGPT's search results, so this is worth more than its traffic suggests.
3. **Apple Business Connect** — businessconnect.apple.com. Free, and it decides what iPhone Maps and Siri say about you. A large share of 65-year-olds use iPhones.
4. **Facebook Page** — a business page, not a personal profile, with the same details and a link to the site. Also where community groups will find you.
5. **Nextdoor Business Page** — the single most relevant social network for this audience and this geography. Neighbors ask for insurance recommendations there constantly.
6. **Yelp** — free listing. Low traffic for insurance, but it's a citation Google reads.

## Tier 2: local links, which is what's actually missing

A handful of genuine local links beats a hundred directory submissions. Aim for ten over the next two months.

- **Greensboro Chamber of Commerce** ([greensboro.org/membership](https://greensboro.org/membership/)) — 1,300+ members, and membership includes a directory listing that links to your site. It costs money, so treat it as a business decision, not just an SEO one; the referrals and the local credibility are the real return.
- **Better Business Bureau of Central North Carolina** — an accredited listing is a strong local citation and the sort of thing this audience checks.
- **Alignable** — free, local-business focused, and gives a followed link.
- **Your church, and any club, gym, or group you belong to** — member and sponsor pages are exactly the kind of local link Google trusts and competitors can't copy.
- **UNCG connections** — student organizations and alumni directories often list members' businesses. Use only what's genuinely yours, and never imply the university endorses your practice.
- **Public library and senior center event pages** — when you run a "Turning 65 in the Triad" talk, the venue lists the event, with a link. This is the best one on the list: it produces a link, an audience, and appointments at the same time.
- **Local news and community calendars** — the Rhino Times, Triad Business Journal community calendar, and neighborhood newsletters list free educational events.

Email to send an organization:

> Subject: Free "Turning 65 and Medicare" talk for your members
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Christian Brinkley, a licensed insurance agent here in Greensboro. I give a short, plain-English talk for people approaching 65 on how Medicare enrollment actually works — the deadlines, what the mail means, and the mistakes that cost people money. There's nothing to buy and I don't collect anything from attendees.
>
> Would that be useful for your members this fall? I can do 30 minutes or an hour, and I'm happy to work around your calendar.
>
> Christian Brinkley
> (336) 365-7422 · christianbrinkleync.com

## Tier 3: insurance directories, with a caveat

Sites like medicareagentshub.com and certifiedmedicareagents.com will list you, and they do pass a link. Read what they do with your information first: several of them exist to resell leads, which is the opposite of the promise your site makes. If a listing requires handing over prospects, skip it. A link is not worth contradicting the one thing that makes you different.

## Reviews: the highest-value thing you can do this week

Reviews decide the order of the map pack, and the map pack is what most "medicare agent near me" searches actually show. You have none. Competitors with forty will outrank a perfect website indefinitely.

- Ask every client you've already helped. Start with the ones whose problem you actually solved.
- Ask in person or by phone, then send the link. Google gives you a short review link in your Business Profile under "Ask for reviews."
- Never offer anything in exchange — it violates Google's policies and CMS marketing rules, and it's the kind of thing that costs an insurance license.
- Specific beats glowing: "He found out my cardiologist wasn't in the plan I was about to pick" persuades a stranger. "Great service" doesn't.

What to say:

> I'm building up my Google reviews, and yours would carry real weight with other families around here. Would you mind writing a couple of sentences about what we worked on? Here's the link: [your review link]

Once three or four are in, they appear on the website automatically — the section is built and stays hidden until real ones exist.

## What not to do

- Don't buy links or pay for "500 directory submissions." Google discounts them at best.
- Don't create a second Google profile. Duplicates get suspended.
- Don't vary the phone number or business name between listings, even slightly.
- Don't write your own reviews or ask staff to. That's the one mistake that can end a listing permanently.
