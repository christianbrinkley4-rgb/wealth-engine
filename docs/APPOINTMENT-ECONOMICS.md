## Appointment economics

Let:

- `A` = total advertising spend during the test.
- `F` = other allocated campaign cash costs, such as hosting, domain, printing, and incremental list costs.
- `N` = qualified appointments actually attended.
- `C` = commission per completed, commissionable enrollment (Christian’s estimate: $400).
- `p` = proportion of attended appointments producing an enrollment. Unknown for this campaign.
- `r` = allowance for chargebacks or commission reductions. Use Christian’s actual experience when available.
- `v` = time, travel, and service cost allocated to each attended appointment, including the cost of follow-up attempts as appropriate.

Marketing cash cost per attended appointment = `(A + F) / N`.

Full allocated cost per attended appointment = `(A + F) / N + v`.

Expected retained commission per attended appointment = `C × p × (1 − r)`.

Expected contribution per appointment = expected retained commission minus full allocated cost.

Expected ROI = expected contribution divided by full allocated cost. This is a planning estimate, before income tax, not a guarantee. For a completed test, use actual commissions retained and actual costs instead of expected fractional enrollments. Commission receipt may occur after the advertising and appointment costs are paid.

At $400 with no allowance for chargebacks or other expenses, closing 10%, 20%, 25%, one third, or 50% of attended appointments yields respectively $40, $80, $100, about $133, or $200 in average commission per appointment. Those values are gross break-even ceilings before the remaining costs, not sensible spending targets.

## An illustration, not a forecast

Assume $300 ad spend, $11.48 first-year domain cost charged to this initial test, free hosting, 25% enrollment conversion, 10% commission reserve, and $25 time/travel allowance per attended appointment. The 25%, 10%, and $25 assumptions are unverified examples. Expected retained commission per appointment is $90.

| 4                     | $77.87                              | $102.87                   | -$12.87                               |
| 6                     | $51.91                              | $76.91                    | $13.09                                |
| 8                     | $38.94                              | $63.94                    | $26.06                                |
| 12                    | $25.96                              | $50.96                    | $39.04                                |

Under those assumptions the test needs **at least five attended appointments for expected break-even**. Actual enrollments will be whole numbers, and even six or eight appointments can produce zero enrollments. With zero enrollments, the entire cash spend and time/travel can be lost.

If 25% enrollment conversion is realistic, an initial **marketing cash-cost target around $40–$50 per attended appointment** leaves room for expenses and uncertainty at a $400 commission. It is a financial target, not a claim that ads will deliver that price. At a 10% enrollment rate, that same target may lose money.

Do not report booked-appointment cost as attended-appointment cost. For example, $40 per confirmed booking becomes about $57.14 per attended appointment with a 70% attendance rate, before any additional costs. The 70% is an example, not a forecast for Christian.

## Spending recommendation

Finish free search setup and the functional booking/capture path first. Then, only if Christian can afford the full downside, run one local T65 campaign with a fixed total cash limit. Do not initially add a mailing campaign and four separate advertising audiences. A very small pilot can expose tracking and quality problems but may not provide enough evidence to declare an A/B winner.

Track spend, source, qualified requests, confirmed appointments, attendance, completed enrollments, received commissions, chargebacks, and time. Pause for broken tracking, irrelevant inquiries, or a breached spending cap. Do not repeatedly reset an unsuccessful pilot’s budget. Increase spending only after repeatable appointment economics and collection timing support it; a few early sales do not establish guaranteed ROI.
